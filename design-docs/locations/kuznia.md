# Estella VI, Kuznia

A cold, heavy, storm-wracked industrial world under a thick N₂/CO₂ sky — and the only planet in the Estella system governed by its own workers. Kuznia is the industrial backbone of the Camps: foundries, machine cities, feedstock intake, and the hardest physical labor in the system. It is operated by the **Steel Combine** — at once a republic, a company, and a commune — and it is the reason the Camps corridor *Belt → Camps → everywhere* runs on collectivized steel.

## How it came to be

### The Concession

Every firm on Hartwell was in the business of expanding outward, and Estella VI was the richest prospect any of them had ever surveyed. It was also the one nobody could take *gradually.* Kuznia's atmosphere is unbreathable and its weather murderous; the planet returns nothing until the **entire** life-support lattice — heat, pressure, and oxygen for a whole industrial workforce — is already standing. You cannot start small and grow into profit here. That made Kuznia a winner-takes-all proposition riding on a single, ruinous, up-front commitment: a great **Gaia loan**, extended to one house, that would either build a planet or bury the borrower. **Ostrander Colonial** — the most successful of Hartwell's crowd of miners and industrialists, pulling steadily ahead of its rivals — won that bet. It staged the push from Hartwell's **Ascension Scaffold**, took the whole world, and called it the **Ostrander Concession**: the venture that would vault it from *leading Hartwell company* to *system-scale power.*

They built it as a company-world. The foundries, the machine city, and — the detail that mattered most — the entire life-support lattice were Ostrander property. On a planet whose air will kill you and whose weather is actively trying to, whoever owns the heat, the pressure, and the oxygen owns the people. Ostrander metered all three against production quota. Fall behind on quota and the company could turn your hab down toward survival minimum. This was the original **quota culture**: a debt-peonage instrument backed by the thermostat.

### The Zorya and the Udarniki

Highliners are weather, not shipping. Nobody schedules them, charters them, or steers them. Roughly three generations ago one such unscheduled arrival — the **Zorya**, out of the **Corliss** system — brought a cargo Ostrander had not asked for and did not understand: work-pilgrims from a wholly communist civilization, laborers whose culture sends out volunteers on a vocation of hard work for humanity. They called themselves **Udarniki**.

They surveyed the system and chose Estella VI as the worthiest worksite in it — the hardest, most-needed industrial labor available — and they came down to *work.* Sincerely. They did not arrive to overthrow anyone; they integrated into Ostrander's operation as its best and most disciplined labor, and in the doing they renamed the world in their own tongue — Kuznia, *the forge* — and stamped forge-gods across the map: Perun, Mokosh, Veles, Morana, Stribog, and the moons Svarog and Kalyna. Renaming was an act of possession, and the name stuck so completely that today even Ostrander's own Hartwell rivals say Kuznia. The first Udarniki worked, and aged, and died under Ostrander's metered air in good faith.

### The Covenant Wars and the Long Winter

The break came a full generation after the Zorya, and it came from outside. Another Highliner committed to Estella — the **Covenant**, carrying religious fanatics on a colonization mission who meant to convert or conquer the whole system. Their arrival touched off the **Covenant Wars**: a period of system-wide disorder — contested landings, sectarian violence, holy raids on "profane" traffic — that made moving cargo anywhere in Estella dangerous and ruinously expensive, and froze ordinary trade.

For worlds that ship in the very air their workers breathe, that was a vise. Kuznia's costs soared — every tonne of imported life-support now carried a war premium — while its revenue collapsed, because with trade frozen nobody was buying steel. And Ostrander was already over-leveraged on the ruinous Gaia loan that had bought the whole planet. Caught between its loan payments and a crisis it could not absorb, it did the only thing a panicking debtor can: it passed the shock down the thermostat, cranking quotas and cutting the workers' heat and air to keep itself solvent.

That was the betrayal that broke faith. It was the Udarniki's **children** — Kuznia-born, raised on the collective ideal but now freezing under a rationed thermostat to service a debt they never signed — who finally rose. The revolt, the **Long Winter**, did not begin with the foundries. It began with **life-support.** Once the councils held the heat, the air, and the pressure, they held the planet, and Ostrander's title deeds became paper.

The pivotal outside actor was the **Teamsters' Guild.** Ostrander demanded the Guild cut fuel to starve the revolt; the Guild, formally neutral and structurally above ideology, kept selling fuel to whoever paid in hard currency — and so the new workers' state survived its first winter. It also learned the permanent lesson that shapes it still: *you can expropriate the foundries and even the air, but you can never expropriate the fuel.*

The Covenant Wars eventually burned out: the fanatics failed to take the system and were driven outward into the Wells, where they hold two moons to this day and trouble the shipping lanes no longer. But the damage at Kuznia was already done, and permanent. Nobody retook the planet — there is no standing military in the Interim to do it with, and, more to the point, nobody bombards their own supply chain, for the system's capital runs on Camps output. The result is a cold, permanent **détente**: the commune is left to its experiment so long as it keeps the finished goods flowing outward and keeps buying Guild fuel. Ostrander — its planet-sized loan called, its Gaia backers burned, its crown-jewel venture gone — retreated one world inward to Hartwell, no longer dominant, merely one of many again, staring outward at the Concession it will not stop calling its own.

## The Steel Combine — republic, company, commune

Kuznia is *operated* by the **Steel Combine**, which is, all at once, three things: a **republic** that governs the planet, a **company** that runs its industry and trades on the open market, and a **commune** that owns everything collectively and holds life-support in common. It took its name from the great foundry it was born in. Its population is now a blend of Udarnik descendants and Estella-local immigrants who came for the one thing the Combine guarantees — **life-support as a commons.** Heat, air, and pressure are rights here, not deductions. That is the moral core no Kuznian will ever surrender, and the reason people keep arriving.

Quota culture survived the revolution, inverted: the plan's quotas are now *theirs*, and an **udarnik** is no longer a founding pilgrim but an honorific for any worker who exceeds quota — a shock-worker, a local hero, the ideal made ordinary and open to all.

The Combine is not an autarky, because nothing in Estella can be. Inside, there are no firms buying and selling — there is the plan; foundries requisition inputs and deliver output to quota, and workers are paid in the commons and in standing rather than market wages. Outside, there is a galaxy that speaks only in hard currency. Between them sits a **trade membrane**, and it is run from orbit:

- **Hammer Station** — bulk feedstock intake and finished-goods export; where the state trade arm buys ore on the open market and sells steel and machinery abroad for the credits that keep the heat on.
- **Anvil Station** — customs, paperwork, and dispatch; the bureaucratic face the commune shows to capitalist counterparties.

The whole machine exists, at bottom, to earn enough hard currency to keep buying the two things Kuznia cannot make: **Guild fuel** and off-world imports. A workers' state that must run a permanent trade surplus to survive — selling cheap and reliably to a market it despises — is a state full of contradictions, which is exactly what keeps it alive and interesting.

### The Gornilo Crucible

The heart of it all is **the Gornilo** (Estella VI.4) — *Gornilo Crucible*, the great smelting-and-heavy-refining works, and the founding foundry the Steel Combine took its identity from. *Gornilo* means crucible, or forge-hearth; it also carries the old idiom *the crucible of the revolution.* Both meanings are earned. It is the beating industrial heart of the planet and the place the commune remembers itself into being.

### The rest of the surface

- **Perun City** (VI.3) — the machine city; finished goods and heavy machinery.
- **Mokosh Lowlands** (VI.6) — agricultural lowlands, bulk food for the commune's tables.
- **Kopanka Mine** (VI.8) — mountain mining, specialty ore, a hard high-altitude pad.
- **Morana Station** (VI.7) — polar weather research; named, aptly, for a goddess of winter and death.
- **Port Stribog** (VI.5) — the weather-gated spaceport; passengers and bulk-food dispatch when Stribog's winds allow.

> **Writer's note:** The Guild maintains a certification landing pad on Kuznia and pays a premium to keep it running — the nastiest controlled-landing environment in the system makes it the natural proving ground for the hardest atmospheric/weather-gated landing certs. It is a contained commercial arrangement and a rare stream of Guild hard currency flowing *into* the commune, not a cultural pillar.

## The moons

Both of Kuznia's moons carry Slavic names — christened in the Udarnik renaming wave, when it was all one arrival — but **neither is part of the commune.** They diverged politically only later, and the names predate the split.

### Svarog

The airless shipyard moon (VIa), named for the forge-god. **Svarog Shipyard** was founded *after* Kuznia had already grown into the Camps' natural transport and industrial hub, when the sustained demand to build and service ships finally justified a yard. It is run by a **single operator** who owns the yard infrastructure and **leases berths and space to many manufacturers** — the luxury marque Kisaragi and others are tenants, not owners. **Veles Terminal** (VIa.2) is its controlled materials-and-logistics airlock; **Black Vein Mine** (VIa.4) feeds specialty rare-alloy and refractory work; **Anchorhold** (VIa.3) is the surface ballast, power, and mooring infrastructure.

> **Writer's note:** Svarog's operator, its history, and its precise relationship to the commune below are left to expand later. The load-bearing facts: single landlord, many tenant manufacturers, not communist.

### Kalyna

The bright red, hematite-dusted specialty moon (VIb) — and a genuine third path between Ostrander's exploitation and Kuznia's revolution. Kalyna was colonized by the **minor partner** in Ostrander's Concession consortium — then called **Merren Colonial**. When the Covenant Wars closed the same vise on it — shipping dear, demand dead, the imported air suddenly unaffordable — Merren did the opposite of Ostrander. Rather than pass the crisis down to its workers, it **absorbed the debt and the losses itself** — liquidating every other holding it owned and running Kalyna at a deficit rather than ration its workers' air. It came out of the wars stripped down to a single moon and deep in the red, but owning the one thing Ostrander threw away: the absolute loyalty of the people on it. So Kalyna never revolted; the company not only survived but slowly flourished, in time shedding its hard industrial name to rebrand itself **Merren Conservatory** for the world it was becoming. In the reversal that defines the moon, the *junior* partner outlived the *senior* one.

Its population today is mostly the descendants of the **more agriculturally-minded Udarniki**, communal in spirit but genuinely fond of the company that never gave them reason to rise. They regard Merren as a kind of benign father, and they regard Kalyna as **Estella's garden in the making** — not bulk food, but **specialty, luxury, art, and beauty**: pharmaceutical horticulture at **Zhitomir Hydroponics** (VIb.3), cultured protein at **Teteriv Broiler Farm** (VIb.2), and boutique luxury aquaculture at **Dnipro Pools** (VIb.4), all sealed beneath the red regolith and handled through **Kalyna Orbital** (VIb.1), the moon's single cold-chain, inspection, and transfer station.

> **Writer's note:** *Merren Conservatory* (formerly Merren Colonial) is a provisional name and Kalyna's fuller treatment is deferred. The load-bearing facts: benevolent former Ostrander minor partner, agriculturally-minded Udarnik population, thriving, garden-of-refinement identity.

## Kuznia and the system

- **Against Hartwell / Ostrander.** The commune and its dispossessed founder are next-door neighbors in the Camps, locked in an asymmetric cold war. Ostrander is the aggrieved, diminished party — pouring its dwindling resources into legal claims and reclamation schemes it can no longer afford, while its own Hartwell rivals pragmatically trade with the commune Ostrander refuses to acknowledge. Its grievance bleeds it; its decline is partly its own obsession.
- **Against Cerberus.** Kuznia and **Cerberus Human Resources** are natural enemies: CHR's entire business is human custody and metered labor, precisely what the Udarniki's children overthrew. The commune refuses CHR custody work, and Kuznia is the obvious sanctuary for anyone fleeing it — a standing engine for defector-smuggling and asylum runs.
- **Under the Guild.** The Combine's deepest vulnerability is the fuel line it can never own. Guild neutrality once saved the revolution; Guild politics could still end it. For Kuznia, the fuel monopoly is both lifeline and knife.

> **Writer's note:** Hartwell (Estella V) and Ostrander Colonial warrant their own document — a crowded capitalist frontier of many firms, with Ostrander as its most storied and most fallen name. The **Covenant Wars** were a system-wide event, and the Covenant's descendants still hold two moons in the Wells; both are deferred for their own treatment and matter here only as the shock that forged the commune. The Steel Combine's internal government is deliberately left thin: it is a People's Republic; we do not need to model its councils.
