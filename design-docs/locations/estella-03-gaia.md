# Gaia — an inhabited world still being terraformed

Updated: 2026-10-22
Status: ongoing-terraforming story approved by Pete. This records lore, not new runtime mechanics.

## Settlement chronology

Dahai was colonized first. It already had breathable air and storm-free latitude belts suitable for constructing floating habitats. Its settlers needed habitation, power, food production and ocean engineering, rather than planetary terraforming. Do not use the rejected volcanic-island settlement explanation.

Gaia required terraforming before general open-air habitation. Its subsequent growth did not wait for the programme to finish. Continents provided room for cities, farms, factories and transport networks; accessible mineral resources supplied industry and construction. Terraforming infrastructure also supplied an industrial foundation for mass settlement.

Dahai has the older settlement history. Gaia became the system's populous industrial and political centre while its own planetary transformation was still underway.

## Present-day Gaia

Most of Gaia is fully habitable. Open-air cities, extensive agriculture and established ecosystems support billions of people. Residents can live their entire lives without encountering a terraforming worksite.

Habitability and completion are different milestones. Gaia achieved civilization-scale habitability long ago; the programme continues toward its intended planetary environmental state. Ordinary life and agriculture do not await a final declaration that the planet is finished.

## The unfinished polar regions

The remaining polar ice sheets are substantially larger than the programme's intended final extent and cover considerable high-latitude land. These regions have:

- Extreme cold and prolonged seasonal darkness.
- Deep ice, unstable thawing ground and poorly developed drainage.
- Little usable soil or established terrestrial ecology.
- Scientific and industrial outposts requiring heated, protected habitation.

They are unsuitable for ordinary unprotected settlement, rather than literally inaccessible to humans. Their air is generally breathable: the atmosphere mixes globally. They are not geographically isolated pockets of an oxygen-poor atmosphere.

Exact ice boundaries, regional temperatures, population figures and completion dates remain unauthored.

## Why completion is slow

The inhabited planet now constrains the terraforming programme. Engineers must protect existing coastal cities, agricultural belts and ocean circulation. Rapid ice loss could flood valuable settled land; polar climate changes could disrupt rainfall far away.

Remaining work includes carefully managed ice retreat, drainage, ground stabilization and gradual ecosystem establishment. Simply increasing global temperatures would endanger the civilization the programme already made possible.

## Institutions, politics and local content

The programme remains an active public and economic institution. Its budget, risks to established farmland and ownership of land still buried beneath the ice are continuing political questions. No particular agency name, faction alignment or ownership regime is established here.

Gaia's existing Polar Science and Weather Research site (`estella-iii-polar-science`) is an active terraforming frontier: climate monitoring, glaciology, engineering crews and experimental ecological restoration. Its presence does not imply a generally uninhabitable Gaia.

The First Dome heritage site concerns Gaia's own early settlement, not priority over Dahai in the system's colonization history.
